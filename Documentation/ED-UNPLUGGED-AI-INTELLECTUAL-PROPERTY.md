# ED Unplugged AI: Intellectual Property and Legal Status

**Purpose:** This document records general intellectual-property
information and the current verification limits for ED Unplugged AI
(also referred to as ED or JR). It is not a legal opinion, a patent
application, a copyright registration, or proof of ownership.

## 1. Copyright

Copyright may protect original human-authored expression fixed in a
tangible form. For this project, potentially protected expression may
include original source code, documentation, graphics, interface
artwork, and other qualifying creative materials.

Copyright does not protect an idea, concept, system, process, method
of operation, or algorithm merely because it is described in code or
documentation. Protection applies to qualifying expression, subject
to applicable law and third-party rights.

In the United States, copyright protection generally begins when
qualifying original authorship is fixed in a tangible medium.
Registration is a separate process. Registration can provide important
procedural and evidentiary benefits.

**Rights-holder verification required:** The legal name of the owner
or owners, the authorship of individual contributions, contractor
agreements, assignments, and any third-party or open-source components
must be reviewed before making a definitive ownership claim or filing
a registration.

Suggested notice format, to use only after confirming the proper
rights holder:

`Copyright © 2026 [CONFIRMED RIGHTS-HOLDER]. All rights reserved.`

This repository documentation does not itself establish that every
component is owned by one person or entity.

## 2. Software and third-party components

ED Unplugged AI may use third-party software, libraries, models,
services, or other materials. Their licenses and terms remain
applicable. A project-level notice cannot override those licenses.

Before distribution, maintain an inventory of dependencies, model
licenses, attribution requirements, notices, and any restrictions on
commercial use or redistribution.

No blanket claim is made that all included or referenced third-party
materials are owned by the ED project.

## 3. Patent status

**No patent application number, filing receipt, or issued patent
information has been supplied or verified for this documentation.**

Accordingly:

- This project is not described here as patent pending.
- No patent number or filing date is claimed.
- A public description or Git commit is not a patent filing.
- A provisional patent application is not an issued patent and is
  generally abandoned after 12 months unless the required follow-on
  filing is made in time.
- Inventorship must be determined from the actual claimed invention,
  not from project ownership, funding, management, or general
  assistance.

If ED contains a potentially patentable technical invention, obtain
advice from a registered patent practitioner before publicly
disclosing its enabling details. Public disclosure can affect patent
rights. U.S. law has a limited grace-period framework in some
circumstances, but foreign patent rights may be lost by pre-filing
disclosure.

## 4. Trademarks and project names

Copyright does not protect names, titles, or short phrases.
Names such as ED Unplugged AI, ED, and JR may require separate
trademark review if they are used as identifiers for products or
services.

No trademark registration or exclusive trademark right is claimed
by this document. Availability and ownership should be checked
before relying on a name as a registered mark.

## 5. Public repository and licensing

Publishing source code or documentation to a public repository makes
the published material publicly accessible. This is not a substitute
for a patent filing or copyright registration.

No new open-source license is granted by this document. Existing
license files and third-party license obligations, if any, must be
read independently. Before adding a project-wide license, the
authorized rights holder should choose the intended permissions.

## 6. Recommended protection and recordkeeping

1. Identify every human author and contributor.
2. Preserve dated source history, design files, drafts, and release
   records.
3. Obtain written assignments or contributor agreements where needed.
4. Inventory third-party code, model weights, datasets, and assets.
5. Identify any potentially patentable inventions before public
   technical disclosure.
6. Consult qualified counsel about copyright registration, patent
   filings, trademark clearance, and ownership.
